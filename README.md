# Introduction

The [BlueCherry platform](https://bluecherry.io) is a versatile, management focused IoT platform
which supports both classic OS (Linux, Windows, OSX) based IoT devices as well as MCU
(nRF, ESP32, STM32, Renesas, ...). In this introduction section you will discover the
functionalities of the platform and the most important concepts.

## Features
The BlueCherry platform is an IoT platform designed from the ground up in Belgium. Five focus
points are used throughout the design of the platform:
  * **Security**: the platform only stores data that is absolutely necessary for it to function
    properly, the GDPR legislation was a guideline throughout the implementation instead of an
    afterthought, every connection is encrypted, CRA compliance is built-in, ...

  * **Speed**: nothing is more cumbersome than slow software. The BlueCherry platform is
    written from the ground up and is not dependent on any larger 3rd party software platform. This
    allowed us to have 100% control over the performance.

  * **Versatility**: every part of the platform can be customized. Both the look and feel can be
    adapted to fit your company needs.

  * **Minimal vendor lock-in**: we won't deny that it will always take some engineering effort when
    you want to migrate between platforms. For the BlueCherry platform however we have created its
    architecture in such a way that vendor lock-in is minimal: no proprietary protocols,
    standardized protocols, no multitude of proprietary managed services.

  * **Transparent cost**: connecting a few devices to any platform is usually not expensive.
    Typically the choice of an IoT platform is made early on in the development process, usually
    the PoC or MVP stage, when it's difficult to estimate the number of connected devices in
    production. It's even harder to estimate the monthly costs involved when scaling your
    system. BlueCherry has an all-in-one formula so you don't need to pick and choose between
    a multitude of services with different usage-based costs. This way you know exactly how much
    your deployment will cost you at scale.

The goal of the BlueCherry platform is *IoT device management*, more specifically:
  * **Connectivity**: devices are deployed in heterogeneous network environments, there are (CG)NAT
    routers, multi-NAT layers, firewalls, proxy-servers, ... The BlueCherry platform makes
    bi-directional communication with your device possible regardless of the situation.

  * **Ownership management**: every device in the platform has one dedicated owner, it's the owner
    who decides what happens with the data generated by that device and who is allowed to access it.

  * **Firmware management**: IoT devices run software which is called firmware. That firmware must
    be updated in the field to fix bugs, add features, keep security up-to-date, ... BlueCherry has
    extensive firmware over-the-air update mechanisms for various platforms.

  * **Data delivery**: devices generate data from sensors, events, internal state, ... that must be
    delivered to the application layer. The BlueCherry platform ensures this is done in the most
    optimal way (for example UDP over NB-IoT). In order to keep your application layer as portable
    as possible the data is always delivered in a standard format (typically JSON over MQTTS) and
    the device will send it over a more optimized link (CoAP + DTLS for lite devices) or stream
    their fully local interface (full devices).

  * **Operational excellence**: as a manufacturer you either have a direct B2B or B2C model. In any
    case you will need to give customer support and customer support needs to have efficient access
    to the metadata of the devices. Therefore the platform offers manufacturers and dealers a
    comprehensive interface in which they can view the ownership, request support access, make
    notes, attach files, initiate device claims, initiate ownership transfers, check operational
    device state and hardware manufacturing data.

  * **Dealer management**: as a manufacturer you can give partial access to your device management
    portal to your dealers. A dealer is typically a distributor, installer, or subdivision. The
    dealers form a tree-like structure beneath the manufacturer.

## Device types
The platform distinguishes two kinds of devices:
  * **Full devices**: whenever the IoT device is powerful enough and a broadband connection is
    available a device will be designed as a *full device*. This means that the full stack, from
    low level hardware drivers to business logic and UI, runs on the device itself. This unique
    approach ensures that the device stays fully operational even without an internet connection.
    In this case the BlueCherry platform is *complementary* and extends the device's
    functionality as soon as an internet connection is available.

    From a technical perspective a *full* device makes use of the **bluecherry-client** to connect
    to the platform. This piece of software, available for Linux, Windows and OSX, connects the
    local web interface of the device to the platform and makes your local device a secure IoT
    device in mere seconds.

  * **Lite devices**: when a device is not capable of running a full stack or when the connection
    doesn't allow 24/7 access to the internal webserver (for example LTE-M, NB-IoT,
    satellite, LoRa, Mioty, ...) the BlueCherry platform can take over the business logic, UI and
    storage of the devices. This is also called the 'classic' IoT approach.

    From a technical perspective a *lite* device connects to the platform via the CoAP + DTLS
    protocol, typically via a [BlueCherry library](https://github.com/bluecherry) implementation.
    This type of connection is currently available for various MCUs and Linux based systems.
